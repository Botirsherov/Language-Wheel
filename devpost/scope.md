# Word Wheel

A web app where learners practice vocabulary with a colorful spinning wheel and a flip-to-reveal translation card.

## The Unique Kernel
Each word is a small recall challenge: spin to choose it, try to remember its translation before flipping the card, then decide whether it was learned. Words marked “I got it” leave the current round; words marked “not yet” stay for more practice. When the round ends, the words return so the learner can practice again.

## Who It's For
People who have trouble learning and remembering vocabulary, including learners practicing English–Spanish, English–French, or English–German words.

## The Core Loop
The learner opens the page, starts with a ready-made word set or adds their own words, spins the wheel, tries to recall the translation, flips the card to check, and marks “I got it” or “not yet.” Correct words leave the current round; missed words remain. At the end, the words return for another round.

## Inspiration & Identity
The central image is a large wheel with colored word segments and an add sign in its center. The page also has titled example-word blocks below the wheel. A word appears on a card that flips to reveal its translation. The learner may choose different colors for words.

## Why This Matters to the Learner
The learner sees that many people struggle to learn words and wants to make a web app to help them practice.

## What "Working" Looks Like
Someone opens a page with a large wheel and example blocks, chooses words or adds their own, spins to get a word, flips its card to reveal the translation, and marks whether they remembered it. Words marked “I got it” leave that round while “not yet” words remain; when the round is over, the words return so they can practice again.

## The POC Boundary
Build one web page with the wheel, word and translation entry, optional word colors, a few ready-made essential-word examples for English–Spanish, English–French, and English–German, the spin-and-flip practice loop, self-marking, and round restart. Keep it focused on demonstrating vocabulary practice; accounts and saved progress are not part of this version.

## Later
Accounts to save progress and return to it later.

## Explicitly Cut
- Accounts and persistent progress, because the learner identified these as a future addition rather than part of the first practice experiment.
